# Quiz 1: worked-solution structure

The filename is retained for existing links. This document now describes the
solution structure, not a printable marking policy. The user clarified on
2026-09-24 that the original percentages were intended to guide the solutions.
The earlier detailed scoring policy is superseded; its history remains in
[WORKLOG.md](WORKLOG.md).

## Current requirements

- Cover all three source variants and all twelve problems in English.
- Copy each problem statement verbatim from its immutable source PDF,
  retaining notation, data, questions and the original 25 marks.
- Keep editorial model assumptions in the solution, not in the statement.
- Use short numbered steps with clear explanations.
- Include a useful graph or diagram; the requested V1/P1 velocity graph is required.
- State the relevant formulas, then derive the final expression symbolically
  before substituting numerical values.
- Give the answer with units and a sensible precision.
- Write mathematical divisions using `\frac`; use compact unit ratios such as
  `m/s` and `m/s²`, as clarified by the user after reviewing the draft.
- Use `Δt` for durations and `a` with explained subscripts for acceleration.
  Newly introduced speeds use `v` with subscripts, and times use `t` or `Δt`;
  preserve notation already supplied in the verbatim source statements.
- For all circular-motion problems, show the method using centripetal
  acceleration `a_c = v²/R`, with a short vector alternative where useful.
- Use ordinary page text: no enclosing solution boxes or framed answers.
- Omit grading tables, percentages, partial-credit instructions and penalties from the PDFs.
- Briefly explain meaningful alternative methods or answer forms without a separate marking section.

## Authoring guide

The original 25% / 25% / 40% / 10% indicate the four aspects to cover:

| Aspect | What the worked solution supplies |
|---|---|
| Graph or explanatory diagram | A readable visual and a short explanation of its physical meaning. |
| Formulas and explanation | The applicable physical laws and definitions. |
| Symbolic derivation | Correct use of those laws and the requested quantity expressed in variables. |
| Numerical result | Substitution, calculation and units. |

These aspects may be combined into three to five natural steps. They are not
printed as scoring categories. Use concise headings such as “Find the velocity”,
“Calculate the displacement”, and “Find the average velocity”.

The two entry points share the same problem statements. The student PDF hides
only the solution content; no additional student instructions are invented.
