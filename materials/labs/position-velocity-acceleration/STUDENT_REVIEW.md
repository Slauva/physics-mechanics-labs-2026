# Novice-student comprehension review

Review scope: the revised Stage 3 student deck and its interleaved solution
deck, checked as material for a Physics I student who has just learned the
definitions of position, velocity, acceleration, derivatives, and integrals.

## Overall result

The 11-slide student deck is understandable at the intended level. The first
two content slides introduce every recurring kinematic symbol, spell out all
units used in the source, and establish the differentiation/integration
relations before the problems begin. Each problem keeps the professor's data
and requested quantities together on one slide. The solution deck shows the
missing algebraic steps and unit conversions without placing solution hints on
the student slides. Each worked calculation establishes its governing equation
before the final answer. Problem 7 intentionally keeps the source coefficients
12 and 2 throughout its derivation instead of replacing them with auxiliary
variables.

No roadmap, extra learning outcomes, generic modeling routine, decision map, or
invented numerical range remains in the presentation.

## Source limitations retained for review

- Problem 2(a) asks for the cheetah's distance, but the professor's answer slide
  gives only the catch time. The solution deck derives the distance directly
  from the stated speed and time and labels this in an instructor note.
- Problem 4 does not state an initial position. The professor's solution uses
  `x(0) = 0`; the redesigned solution states that assumption explicitly, while
  the student prompt remains unchanged.
- Problem 6 does not provide a numerical value of gravitational acceleration.
  The redesigned solution uses `g = 9.8 m/s²`, consistent with the professor's
  numerical answers; it is not added to the student prompt.
- Problem 5 is a graphical reading task. Its numerical values should be read as
  approximate, not as measurements from an analytic function.

These are inherited source ambiguities, not new presentation content. They
should be resolved or explicitly accepted during Stage 4 content verification.
